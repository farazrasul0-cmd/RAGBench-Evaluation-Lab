Tumor angiogenesis induced by excessive vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) results in structurally abnormal, hyperpermeable blood vessels and elevated interstitial fluid pressure that impairs drug delivery. Judicious low-dose anti-VEGF therapy transiently normalizes the tumor vasculature, pruning immature vessels, enhancing pericyte coverage, improving tumor perfusion and oxygenation, and synergistically enhancing radiation and chemotherapeutic delivery.
