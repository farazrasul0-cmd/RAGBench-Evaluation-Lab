Neomorphic heterozygous mutations in isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1 R132H) alter the catalytic active site, converting alpha-ketoglutarate to the oncometabolite D-2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG). Pathological accumulation of 2-HG competitively inhibits alpha-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases, including TET family 5-methylcytosine hydroxylases and KDM histone demethylases, producing genome-wide hypermethylation (G-CIMP phenotype).
